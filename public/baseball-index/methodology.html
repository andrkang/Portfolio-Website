The central idea

Separate player quality from card price.

The full model produces two headline scores. The Hobby Index estimates a prospect’s long-term baseball and collector profile. The Investment Rating asks whether a standardized raw 1st Bowman autograph price offers sufficient value for that profile.

01Measure

Normalize scouting, performance, role, and risk.

02Project

Combine baseball ability with hobby appeal.

03Value

Estimate career paths and price each outcome.

04Decide

Compare decision value with the current card price.

Why both scores matter

An elite prospect can be a poor investment when the market price already assumes stardom. A less famous player can offer better value when the entry price leaves a larger margin of safety.

Step 1 · 0–100

Scouting percentile

Raw 20–80 tool grades are normalized by role and tool, converted to a common scale, and compared with a fixed historical FanGraphs Top 100 reference cohort.

normalized tool = clamp(50 + 10 × (grade − tool mean) / tool SD, 20, 80)
converted tool = (normalized tool − 20) / 60 × 100

Hitter composite = 45% Offense + 20% Athletic/Defense
                 + 20% FV + 15% Star Ceiling

Pitcher composite = 25% Fastball + 25% Best Secondary
                  + 15% Other Secondaries + 20% Command
                  + 15% Starter Projection

Inputs: wRC+, strikeout rate, ISO, player age, and league age.

Inputs: K%, BB%, FIP or ERA, workload, player age, and league age.

Step 3 · Higher is safer

Risk safety

Risk Safety = 100 − Injury Penalty − Prospect Risk Penalty
            − Performance Risk Penalty − Role Penalty
            − Distance-to-MLB Penalty

For hitters, performance risk primarily reflects strikeout rate. For pitchers, it primarily reflects walk rate. Distance penalties increase from MLB/Triple-A toward the lower minor leagues.

Steps 4–5

Baseball Future and Hobby Index

Hitter Baseball Future = 60% Scouting + 30% Performance + 10% Risk

Pitcher Baseball Future = 40% Scouting + 30% Performance
                        + 20% Projected MLB Role + 10% Risk

Hobby Relevance = 40% Star Style + 25% Team Appeal
                + 20% Position/Role Appeal + 15% Marketability

Hobby Index = 70% Baseball Future + 30% Hobby Relevance

Marketability combines name recognition, personal story, charisma/visibility, and fanbase reach. These are explicit assumptions in the full calculator and remain editable rather than being presented as objective facts.

Historical comparison

Career-outcome probabilities

The model compares each prospect with mature historical FanGraphs profiles using prospect-era tools, FV, rank, age, and position. Similarity-weighted outcomes become the starting probabilities, then small-sample estimates are pulled toward historical base rates.

similarity weight = exp((similarity − 100) / temperature)

smoothed probability =
  (neighbor outcome weight + historical rate × prior strength)
  / (neighbor count + prior strength)

Card-market model

Outcome value and present value

Each projected career path is paired with resolved-player card-price evidence. The model blends arithmetic and downside-sensitive geometric values, allows for selling costs, and discounts distant outcomes.

Expected Value = Σ(path probability × path price)
Downside-Aware Value = exp(Σ(probability × ln(path price)))
Gross Outcome Value = 70% Downside-Aware + 30% Expected
Net Present Value = Gross Value × (1 − 15% costs) / 1.12^holding years

Final 0–100 score

Investment Rating

Valuation Score = clamp(50 + 30 × ln(NPV / Current Price))

Investment Rating = 40% Hobby Index
                  + 50% Valuation Score
                  + 10% Confidence

Confidence measures the amount and relevance of the supporting probability, price, and comparable-player evidence. It is not the probability that the forecast is correct.

Responsible use

Limits and interpretation

  • This is a screening model, not a guarantee or personalized financial advice.
  • Prospect development, injuries, card condition, scarcity, grading, fees, and collector trends can overwhelm the estimate.
  • Historical samples—especially rare ace and superstar outcomes—are small.
  • Current prices and scouting reports update on different schedules.
  • The GitHub Pages ranking is a dated snapshot, not a live market feed.
